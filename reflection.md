# Real-World Collaboration Initiative — Reflection

---

## Scope of Initiative

*For the written portion, clearly define in 1–2 paragraphs the scope of your initiative.*

HAAG researchers work on computer science problems that often have real-world applications. Some of those projects naturally evolve into external partnerships with cities, nonprofits, and organizations. This initiative documents and systematizes that partnership process—creating a replicable framework for how academic research can collaborate with real-world partners to achieve meaningful impact.

The Real-World Collaboration Initiative provides HAAG faculty and researchers with a documented procedure for identifying collaboration opportunities, scoping partnerships with external organizations, building sustainable relationships, executing collaborative research, and measuring real-world impact. Using the Charleston Trains case study as our proof of concept, the initiative provides practical guidance at each phase, from initial exploratory conversations through partnership conclusion and knowledge sharing with the broader research community.

## Alignment with HAAG's Goals

*What evidence do you have that your initiative aligned with HAAG's goals? (Conversations with HAAG members, observations)*

There is already a successful example of real-world collaboration within HAAG that has motivated the research direction of one of the group's teams, demonstrating that this kind of work is both feasible and aligned with how HAAG operates. To ground this initiative in that experience, I conducted an interview with Prof. Mussmann, the faculty lead on that collaboration. He provided insights into how the partnership unfolded, what made it work, and how it fit within HAAG's broader research goals. He also reviewed drafts of this initiative and offered feedback that shaped the procedure—particularly around the early scoping phases and the importance of establishing clear expectations with external partners from the outset.

## Procedures Generated

*What procedures did you generate? What is the purpose of the procedure? Provide a link.*

The Real-World Collaboration Initiative generated two procedures.

### Procedure 1: Real-World Collaboration Procedure

This is the core deliverable — a five-phase framework that guides HAAG research projects through partnership development:

1. **Identifying Opportunities** – Determining when a research project has genuine partnership potential (Go/No-Go criteria)
2. **Scoping Partnerships** – Defining specific, bounded problems with measurable outcomes and clear expectations
3. **Building Relationships** – Establishing institutional buy-in, formal agreements, and communication structures
4. **Executing the Partnership** – Managing active research with ongoing communication and iteration
5. **Measuring Impact** – Documenting outcomes and sharing learnings with the research community

The purpose is to give HAAG faculty and researchers a step-by-step framework for moving from interesting research ideas to meaningful real-world partnerships. It is grounded in the Charleston Trains case study and addresses the gap many academic researchers face: how to systematically engage with external organizations in ways that strengthen both research and real-world outcomes.

To support Phase 1 of this procedure, the initiative also maintains a **Partnership Opportunities Directory** — a curated list of cities, civic tech organizations, regional infrastructure bodies, academic partners, and funding sources that are natural fits for HAAG research. Research groups are encouraged to consult this directory when exploring potential collaborators.

**Link:** [website/procedure.html](website/procedure.html) | [Partnership Opportunities Directory](website/opportunities.html)

### Procedure 2: Website Maintenance & Initiative Leadership

This procedure covers the ongoing stewardship of the initiative itself — how the website is maintained and how leadership of the initiative transitions over time. It defines the role of the Initiative Lead (keeping the partner directory current, running the annual procedure review, maintaining the partnership registry, and serving as the primary point of contact for new partnership inquiries), specifies what a clean leadership handoff entails (documentation walkthrough, partner introductions, communication transfer, and open items briefing), and ensures the initiative can continue beyond any single person's tenure. The procedure is documented in the Governance & Integration page.

**Link:** [website/governance.html](website/governance.html)

## Procedure Evaluation

*Each procedure will be evaluated for the following:*

### The intended audience is clear

- **Collaboration procedure:** Audience explicitly stated on the homepage ("Who This Is For") with a clear hierarchy: HAAG Faculty are the primary audience, followed by HAAG Researchers, Cities & Organizations, and Other Universities. This is restated at the top of [procedure.html](website/procedure.html)
- **Website/leadership procedure:** Audience is HAAG managers and faculty considering taking over the initiative; addressed directly in the "Continuation & Handoff" section of [governance.html](website/governance.html)

### The amount of detail provided is appropriate for the intended audience

- **Collaboration procedure:** Five phases, each with key questions, decision checkpoints, step-by-step guidance, and common challenges — detailed enough for faculty to act on, without assuming prior partnership experience
- **Website/leadership procedure:** Covers the Initiative Lead role, what the role owns, and a structured handoff checklist — appropriately concise for an internal transition document

### The procedure is complete without outside context and is reproducible

- **Collaboration procedure:** Each phase specifies inputs, decisions, and outputs; includes embedded templates (scope document outline, agreement checklist, communication plan, impact documentation guide)
- **Website/leadership procedure:** Self-contained handoff steps (documentation walkthrough, partner introductions, communication transfer, open items briefing) that a new lead could follow without prior involvement

### The procedure is realistic with the amount of effort requested by the audience

- **Collaboration procedure:** The timeline spans Weeks 1–12+, but the actual hands-on effort at each phase is typically only a few hours — the calendar span reflects waiting periods, partner scheduling, and institutional processes rather than continuous work. The initiative further reduces burden on faculty and researchers by providing the framework structure, document templates, and initial bullet points for key deliverables, so they are filling in context rather than starting from scratch. "No-go" decisions are encouraged when fit is poor, and a scope creep section manages overextension.
- **Website/leadership procedure:** Handoff is designed to be low-friction — documentation is self-contained and a structured transition can be arranged on request

### The procedures have provided a clear mechanism for integration and enforcement of the process into HAAG's existing structure

- **Collaboration procedure:** [governance.html](website/governance.html) defines decision authority at each phase, a partnership registry, quarterly oversight reviews, semi-annual community reports, and an annual procedure review
- **Website/leadership procedure:** The Initiative Lead role is formally defined with named responsibilities; succession is built into the governance structure rather than left to chance
