What Is This Initiative?

Some HAAG research projects have natural external applications: problems that are more interesting with real data, questions that matter more when a city or organization depends on the answer. When that potential exists, this initiative helps faculty pursue it by identifying the right partners, scoping the collaboration, navigating institutional requirements, and sustaining the work.

The framework is built from a working example. Prof. Mussmann's collaboration with the City of Charleston, predicting train crossing blockages to optimize emergency response and traffic flow, is the proof of concept. That partnership is ongoing, and the lessons from it are documented here.

Why This Matters

Real-world partnerships strengthen research. They provide authentic data, grounded problems, and practical feedback loops that improve both the science and its impact. But there's rarely a clear path from "this could be applied" to "we have a functioning partnership." This initiative fills that gap:

  • A documented process for identifying when a project is ready for external collaboration
  • A replicable framework for scoping, launching, and sustaining partnerships
  • Real-world lessons from a successful case study
  • Guidance on funding collaborative research through grants and cost-sharing

The Charleston Trains Project

Prof. Mussmann and HAAG researchers are collaborating with the City of Charleston to predict train crossing blockages and optimize traffic flow for residents, transit, and emergency vehicles. The connection came through Partnership for Innovation (PIN), which matched the City's challenge with HAAG's research strengths. The data sharing agreement took several months to finalize-an often-underestimated part of real-world partnerships-but the collaboration is ongoing and producing publishable results with practical deployment on the horizon.

Key lessons from this partnership: mutual benefit matters more than formal structure; government partners are often IP-friendly; and the relationship outlasts the project. Prof. Mussmann is now exploring a second collaboration with the same City contact.
