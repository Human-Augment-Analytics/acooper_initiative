Governance & Integration

How the Real-World Collaboration Procedure integrates into HAAG's existing governance structure. This document describes decision authority, oversight, and how the initiative fits within OMSCS program goals.

Decision Authority & Approval

Partnership Approval Levels

Phase 1

Go/No-Go: Pursue scoping?

Identifying Opportunities

Authority: Faculty advisor + Initiative lead
Outcome: Approved or deferred

Phase 2

Go/No-Go: Scope approved?

Scoping Partnerships

Authority: Faculty advisor + Initiative lead + Department leadership
Outcome: Scope signed off or reshaped

Phase 3

Institutional Approval: Agreements finalized?

Building Relationships & Securing Buy-In

Authority: Georgia Tech OTT + HAAG leadership + External partner
Outcome: Formal partnership agreement signed

Phase 4+

Ongoing Oversight

Executing the Partnership / Measuring Impact

Authority: Initiative lead (with faculty advisor)
Decision: Quarterly: on track? Need course correction?

Who Approves What

Initiative Lead: Final authority on partnership fit and phase progression. Manages quarterly reviews, ensures procedure is followed, escalates to HAAG faculty leadership as needed.

Faculty Advisor: Recommends partnership fit, maintains academic standards, manages student team, assesses scope feasibility.

HAAG Leadership: Reviews partnerships for strategic alignment, approves budget commitments, ensures Georgia Tech policy compliance.

Georgia Tech Office of Technology Transfer: Reviews IP and licensing agreements, advises on data confidentiality, drafts formal agreements.

Partnership Registry

All partnerships should be registered in a HAAG partnership database to track outcomes, identify patterns, and share lessons. Each record should include: partner organization, faculty advisor, research team lead, start date, research focus, expected duration, current phase and status, and (at completion) publications, tools delivered, and lessons learned.

Maintenance

  • Quarterly: Initiative lead updates active partnership status
  • Annually: HAAG leadership reviews all partnerships for alignment and impact
  • Semi-annually: Summary report shared with HAAG community

Integration with HAAG & OMCS

Partnerships support the OMCS program by providing real-world research experience for students, grounding research in authentic problems, and demonstrating return on investment for funding bodies. New faculty and researchers should receive a briefing on this procedure as part of onboarding.

Annual Review

The procedure itself should be reviewed annually: gather feedback from faculty, researchers, and partners; review the registry for patterns; assess alignment with HAAG strategy; update and communicate changes.

Risk Management

Scope Creep

Mitigate with a clear scope document and explicit "Phase 2" proposals for expansions. Initiative lead monitors during quarterly reviews.

Data Security / Confidentiality Breach

Georgia Tech OTT reviews all agreements upfront. Partnership agreement specifies data handling. Quarterly reviews include a data security check-in.

Partnership Fails to Deliver Value

Phase 2 defines success metrics upfront. Phase 4 includes quarterly go/no-go assessments. Wind down honestly rather than pushing through.

Faculty/Researcher Overcommitment

Phase 1 explicitly assesses capacity. If an issue emerges, adjust scope or add team members rather than overextending.

Partner Drops Out

Regular communication catches relationship issues early. Partnership agreement includes exit clauses and transition planning.

Non-Negotiable Steps

  • Phase 1 Go/No-Go before investing time in scoping
  • Phase 2 Scope Approval before formal agreements are drafted
  • Phase 3 Formal Agreement before data access or budget commitment
  • Phase 4 Quarterly Reviews for ongoing oversight

Continuation & Handoff

This initiative was developed as an OMCS capstone project and is intentionally designed for continuity. The full procedure, partner directory, and case study documentation are ready for a successor to pick up without starting from scratch.

Initiative Lead Transition

When the current initiative lead transitions out, the incoming lead should be identified and briefed before the transition completes. A clean handoff includes:

Documentation review: Walkthrough of the full procedure, governance structure, and partnership registry

Partner introductions: Warm handover to any active partner contacts, including Charleston and any organizations in active discussion

Communication transfer: Access to shared folders, email threads, and any ongoing correspondence

Open items briefing: Status of any active partnerships, pending outreach, or unresolved decisions

For HAAG Managers Interested in Taking Over

If you're a HAAG manager or faculty member interested in continuing or expanding this initiative, reach out to the current initiative lead at acooper90@gatech.edu. No prior involvement is required. The documentation is designed to be self-contained, and a structured handoff can be arranged.

Procedure Ownership

Whoever holds the Initiative Lead role owns the procedure. That means keeping the partner directory current, running the annual procedure review, maintaining the partnership registry, and serving as the primary point of contact for new partnership inquiries. The role can be held by a faculty member, a senior researcher, or a designated HAAG manager.
