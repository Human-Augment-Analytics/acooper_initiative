Partnership Opportunities Directory

Organizations we've identified as natural partners for HAAG research-either because they're actively seeking research support or because their work aligns well with topics we explore. If you're a researcher considering external collaboration, use this directory to identify potential partners and reach out to discuss outreach strategy.

Cities & Municipalities

Organization Focus Area Why Partner?
City of Atlanta Smart cities, traffic, public safety, equity Georgia Tech's founding MetroLab partner since 2015. Active smart corridor research ongoing. The City's Office of Innovation Delivery and Performance runs technology pilot programs and has a track record of co-producing research with GT.
Contact: Office of Innovation Delivery and Performance
City of Charleston Transportation, infrastructure, emergency response Active HAAG partner on train crossing prediction and traffic optimization. Proven, IP-friendly collaboration through PIN. Read the case study →
Contact: Department of Engineering & Planning
Mid-size Georgia Cities
Savannah, Augusta, Macon		 Civic data, planning, public services Smaller budgets but agile decision-making and often acute need for analytical support. Good fit for scoped, lower-overhead partnerships.
Contact: City planning or IT department, or via the Georgia Municipal Association

Regional Infrastructure & Transit

Organization Focus Area Why Partner?
Georgia Dept. of Transportation (GDOT) Statewide transportation infrastructure, smart corridors, data GDOT sponsors research through the Georgia Transportation Institute (GTI), a Tier I University Transportation Center headquartered at Georgia Tech. Annual Research Needs Statement calls (typically August–September) are open to all GTI member universities. Well-established pipeline for transportation research.
Contact: Georgia Transportation Institute
MARTA Transit operations, equity, multimodal systems Georgia Tech has an active research partnership with MARTA, most recently on the NSF-funded MARTA Reach on-demand transit pilot, which grew to 8,300+ passengers and continues to generate research on multimodal routing and equity. Well-positioned for applied ML work on ridership, equity, and routing optimization.
Contact: MARTA Reach project page
Atlanta Regional Commission (ARC) Regional planning, data, transportation, equity ARC is the metropolitan planning organization for the 11-county Atlanta region. It maintains active research partnerships with Georgia Tech through the Center for Urban Research and publishes open data and dashboards across transportation, population, and land use. Good entry point for regional-scale research.
Contact: Research & Innovation Team
The Ray Highway Sustainable infrastructure, mobility innovation, sensor deployment An 18-mile stretch of I-85 in West Georgia functioning as a living transportation laboratory. The Ray operates via a public-private-philanthropic model (with GDOT and FHWA) and has deployed novel sensor systems, EV infrastructure, and environmental monitoring. Ideal for field-testing applied research at scale.
Contact: contactus@theray.org

Facilitation & Partnership Networks

Organization Focus Area Why Partner?
Partnership for Inclusive Innovation (PIN) University-community research matching, Georgia & Southeast PIN is how the Charleston partnership was formed. Based at Georgia Tech, it connects researchers with local governments through competitive grants, project management support, and a statewide community network. Since 2020 it has supported 220+ projects across 300+ communities with 200+ partners. The Community Research grant program specifically funds university and local government pairs on real-world problems.
Contact: pingeorgia.org
MetroLab Network City-university research (national) Georgia Tech is a founding member (announced at the 2015 White House Smart Cities Forum). MetroLab matches universities with city governments across the US for collaborative research. Membership provides access to peer institutions, cross-city knowledge sharing, and national visibility for published work.
Contact: Atlanta Profile
Civic Tech Atlanta
(formerly Code for Atlanta)		 Civic technology, open government, community tools Atlanta's civic tech volunteer community. Organizes monthly Civic Action Nights and builds open-source tools for local challenges. Good for community-engaged research, rapid prototyping with real users, and connecting with civic practitioners who have direct operational knowledge.
Contact: codeforatlanta.org

Research & Knowledge Partners

Organization Focus Area Why Partner?
Data-Smart City Solutions
Harvard Kennedy School		 Urban analytics, AI in government, civic data best practices Harvard Kennedy School's central resource for cities applying data and technology to urban challenges. Publishes research on AI in governance, predictive analytics, open data, and equity. Run by the Civic Analytics Network. Good for situating HAAG research in the national discourse and peer validation.
Contact: datasmart.hks.harvard.edu
Urban@UW
University of Washington		 Smart cities, data equity, city-university research models A cross-disciplinary UW initiative and MetroLab partner with established city-university research infrastructure (the Seattle MetroLab Studio). Conducts NSF-funded research on mobility, health, and resilience. A strong peer institution for collaboration on national-scale research or methodological knowledge sharing.
Contact: urban.uw.edu/contact
Georgia State University Urban studies, public policy, data science Fellow MetroLab founder (with Georgia Tech and the City of Atlanta). GSU's Urban Studies program and data science faculty offer complementary expertise, particularly on Atlanta neighborhoods, equity, and public policy. Proximity makes collaboration logistically easy.
Contact: Urban Studies or Computer Science departments
Emory University Public health, equity, urban wellness Emory has active joint projects with Georgia Tech and has partnered with the City of Atlanta on the ACES technology ecosystem initiative. Their Rollins School of Public Health is strong on health-data integration, relevant for HAAG research touching health outcomes, environmental equity, or social determinants of urban conditions.
Contact: Rollins School of Public Health

Funding Sources

Source Type Relevance
NSF Civic Innovation Challenge (CIVIC) Federal grant (two-stage competition) Directly funds university-community partnerships piloting solutions to climate resilience and civic resource challenges. Stage 1: up to $75K for planning. Stage 2: up to $1M for execution. CIVIC 3.0 (2024–2026) is the current cohort. Strong fit for applied HAAG projects with a government partner already identified.
Apply at: nsfcivicinnovation.org
NSF Smart and Connected Communities (S&CC) Federal grant (up to $1.5M) Funds interdisciplinary research connecting computing, social science, and city operations. Budget cap recently increased to $1.5M. Accepts supplemental Transition to Practice funding. One of the most direct federal fits for HAAG urban computing research with a city partner.
Apply at: NSF S&CC Solicitation
Georgia Research Alliance (GRA) State-level (grants, loans, co-funding) Nonprofit connecting Georgia's nine research universities with industry and government. Provides grants up to $250K for prototype development and commercialization. Has generated $2.1B in investment and 150+ companies since 1990. Good for early-stage partnership development and co-funding with an industry or civic partner.
Apply at: gra.org
PIN Community Research Grants Program grant (year-long pilots) PIN's Community Research grant program funds year-long pilots pairing university researchers with local governments. Competitive but accessible, with hands-on project management and peer network support. The Charleston partnership was facilitated by PIN and it represents the most direct path for HAAG researchers identifying a new community partner.
Apply at: pingeorgia.org

Work With HAAG

For HAAG Researchers

Identify which organizations above align with your research, then reach out to discuss outreach strategy. We can help facilitate introductions and partnership development. Not sure where to start? Browse by focus area or read the procedure for how partnerships typically develop.

For Cities, Companies & Nonprofits

If you have a research challenge that might align with HAAG's work, we're interested in hearing about it. We collaborate selectively and not every project becomes a partnership, but we're happy to have an exploratory conversation with no commitment.

What we look for

  • Research alignment: Does your challenge connect to urban computing, civic data, smart systems, or related areas?
  • Mutual benefit: Can both sides genuinely gain from the collaboration?
  • Defined scope: Can we identify a specific, bounded problem with measurable outcomes?
