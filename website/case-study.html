Charleston Trains Case Study

One example of how HAAG researchers partnered with a real city to tackle an infrastructure challenge. This demonstrates our approach to collaboration: strategic, focused, and genuinely helpful. It's not every HAAG project that becomes an external partnership—we share this one because it shows what's possible when the fit is right.

The Problem

Train crossing blockages in Charleston, SC create cascading effects throughout the city:

  • Traffic congestion on major thoroughfares
  • Delayed emergency response times
  • Frustrated commuters and business impact
  • Inefficient traffic signal timing

The City of Charleston had been seeking data-driven approaches to predict these blockages and optimize traffic flow, but lacked the in-house computational expertise to build predictive models from their operational data.

The Partnership

HAAG's Role

Develop a predictive model for train crossing blockages using:

  • Real-time transportation data feeds
  • Historical train operational records
  • Emergency response call logs
  • Traffic signal timing patterns

Charleston's Role

Enable the research partnership by providing:

  • Real-world data access and operational context
  • Domain expertise and problem validation
  • Deployment infrastructure and testing environments
  • Regular feedback on research relevance and feasibility

The Outcome

A collaboration that is advancing both academic understanding of urban infrastructure challenges and the City's operational capabilities. The research produced:

  • A validated predictive model for train blockage likelihood
  • Actionable insights for traffic management strategies
  • A reusable framework for other cities facing similar challenges
  • Peer-reviewed publications advancing the field
  • A blueprint for university-city research partnerships

What Made This Work

✓ Mutual Benefit

Both partners gained valuable outcomes. HAAG accessed rich, real-world data and authentic research questions. Charleston received research-backed insights they could actually implement.

✓ Clear Scope

The project had a specific, bounded problem with measurable outcomes. This clarity kept the research focused and deliverables concrete.

✓ Respect for Context

HAAG understood the City's constraints—budget limitations, political realities, operational timelines—and adapted academic rigor to fit real-world constraints.

✓ Regular Communication

Consistent touchpoints between faculty, students, and city partners kept everyone aligned on progress, challenges, and priorities.

✓ Iterative Refinement

Both teams were willing to adapt as understanding evolved. Early assumptions were tested and revised based on real data.

Key Lessons for Future Partnerships

  1. Start small, think big. A focused pilot builds trust and credibility faster than proposing a large, complex multi-year commitment.
  2. Listen more than you talk. External partners understand their problems deeply; academic researchers should ask questions before proposing solutions.
  3. Be transparent about constraints. Neither academic timelines nor government budgets are infinitely flexible. Set realistic expectations upfront.
  4. Celebrate progress, not perfection. Incremental improvement in real-world outcomes often beats waiting for the ideal solution.
  5. Build relationships, not just projects. The strongest partnerships outlast individual research initiatives and evolve as needs change.
