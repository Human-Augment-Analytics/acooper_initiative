Frequently Asked Questions

Have questions about HAAG partnerships? Find answers to common questions below. Can't find what you're looking for? Get in touch.

Partnership Timeline & Commitment

Q: How long does a typical partnership take to set up?
From initial conversation to active research: typically 2-4 months for scope definition, institutional agreement, and project launch. But the conversation matters most—start there with no pressure or commitment. We've found that a 2-4 week exploratory phase where both sides really understand the problem and opportunity is worth the time investment.
Q: Do we need to commit to a multi-year project?
No. Pilots and 1-2 year engagements are welcome and often preferred for a first partnership. We find that successful pilots often evolve into longer-term partnerships naturally. The key is mutual benefit and clear scope, not predetermined duration.
Q: What if our timeline is really short?
Let's talk about it. While research takes time, we can sometimes provide consulting or rapid-cycle analysis for urgent questions. We're flexible and creative about matching our engagement to real constraints.
Q: Can this work for nonprofits or grassroots organizations?
Absolutely. Some of our most impactful work happens at smaller scale with community-based partners and nonprofits. The principles are the same as with larger organizations: clear problem definition, mutual benefit, and regular communication.

Data, IP & Logistics

Q: How do we protect intellectual property and data?
We work with Georgia Tech's Office of Technology Transfer to establish clear IP agreements upfront. Data security and privacy are built in from the start. We handle FERPA, HIPAA, municipal data confidentiality, and other requirements. Each partnership agreement specifies ownership, publication rights, and data handling protocols.
Q: Will you keep our data secure?
Yes. Georgia Tech has institutional infrastructure for secure data handling, and we're happy to discuss specific requirements and compliance needs. We can work with your IT departments to ensure data never leaves secure environments or use anonymization and synthetic data approaches.
Q: What happens to research findings?
This is negotiated as part of the partnership agreement. Typically, partners agree on: (1) Publication timeline and confidentiality periods, (2) Right to review for sensitive information, (3) Joint authorship or acknowledgment, (4) Whether results are public or confidential. We're flexible here because we understand organizations have different needs.
Q: Can we require the research to be kept confidential?
Yes, though this is sometimes in tension with academic norms. We can negotiate confidentiality periods (e.g., 6-12 months post-completion before publication) or restrict publication to certain venues. The key is being upfront about expectations early in conversations.
Q: What if our data is sensitive or proprietary?
We work with sensitive data regularly and have protocols in place. We can work with anonymized or synthetic data, conduct analysis on your infrastructure, or work within specific compliance frameworks. Let's discuss your constraints—we'll find a path forward.

Funding & Budget

Q: What does a partnership cost?
It depends on scope, duration, and required resources. Some partnerships are funded through research grants (NSF, foundations). Others involve cost-sharing or direct funding from the organization. We recommend starting with a conversation about resources and expectations—there are often more options than you might think.
Q: Can HAAG take on a project if we can't pay?
Sometimes, especially if the research aligns with HAAG's mission and funding. We prioritize partnerships that are mutually beneficial. If there's limited budget, we might scope differently or seek grant funding together. Have the conversation—don't assume it's a blocker.
Q: Are there funding sources available?
Yes. NSF programs like Civic Data Science and Smart Communities fund relevant research. State and local grants may be available. Georgia Research Alliance can sometimes co-fund partnerships. We can help identify opportunities and support grant applications.
Q: Can we fund specific grad students or postdocs?
Yes, this is a common model. Partnerships often fund dedicated student or postdoc positions, which gives the research team continuity and your organization a direct point of contact for collaboration.

Research Fit & Scope

Q: How do you decide if a partnership is a good fit?
We consider: (1) Does it align with HAAG's research strengths and focus areas? (2) Is there genuine mutual benefit? (3) Is the problem specific and measurable? (4) Are both partners committed to the collaboration? (5) Are resources (data, time, funding) realistically available? Fit is important—we'd rather say "not right now" than take on projects where we can't deliver value.
Q: What if our problem doesn't fit a research timeline?
Let's talk about it. Sometimes HAAG can serve in a consulting or advisory role rather than as a research partnership. Other times, we can help you find other resources or organizations better suited to your immediate needs. We'd rather be honest than pretend we're the right fit.
Q: Can you do applied work or are you only interested in basic research?
We're very interested in applied work and real-world impact. That's the whole point of HAAG partnerships. We integrate basic and applied research—often, the best research is both rigorous and immediately useful.
Q: What if we're not sure if we need research?
That's fine. We can do a brief exploratory conversation where we help you think through whether research makes sense for your challenge, what questions to ask, and what resources exist. Not every challenge needs research—some need better tooling, policy changes, or just different organizational practices.

People, Teams & Capacity

Q: Will grad students work on this?
Yes, HAAG partnerships often involve grad students as the primary research team, with faculty oversight and mentorship. This is actually ideal—students bring energy and creativity, and partnerships give them real-world research experience. It's part of their education.
Q: Will we work with the same team throughout?
We try to maintain continuity with a core team, especially for longer partnerships. However, students graduate and roles change. We build in transitions and documentation to minimize disruption. For longer partnerships, dedicating a specific postdoc or senior student can provide stability.
Q: How much time commitment will this require from our team?
This varies greatly by project. We try to minimize burden on partners while ensuring we understand the problem deeply. Typically expect: monthly check-ins, quarterly in-depth reviews, and availability for data access/questions. Some projects require more intensive partnership, others less. We'll scope this upfront.
Q: Can we have someone embedded in your team or vice versa?
Absolutely. We've had partners embed staff in research teams and vice versa. This often makes collaboration much more effective. It's something we can discuss and arrange as part of partnership planning.

Success & Outcomes

Q: How do you measure success?
This should be defined together at the start. Success might mean: Research published in peer-reviewed venues, Tools deployed and used, Policy informed, Capacity built in partner organization, Real-world problem improved, or Relationship established for future work. Different partnerships have different definitions.
Q: What if the research doesn't lead to the expected outcomes?
This is normal and not a failure. Sometimes research reveals that assumptions were wrong, the problem is more complex than expected, or a different approach is needed. We treat this as learning and adapt accordingly. Good partnerships are iterative.
Q: Will research results be shared back with us?
Yes, absolutely. Regular reporting, interim findings, and final results are all part of the partnership. We're interested in your feedback and using it to shape the research. You're not just funding research—you're guiding it.
Q: Can we expect recommendations or specific solutions?
Research provides evidence and insights, which we translate into recommendations where appropriate. However, researchers can't typically dictate solutions—that's your domain. We provide the analysis, you make the decisions about implementation.

General Questions

Q: Can you help us think through a problem even if we decide not to partner?
Yes. One conversation might save you months of work or help you realize you need a different kind of support. We're happy to help with problem framing, identifying resources, or making introductions.
Q: How do I get in touch?
Head to our Contact page to reach the HAAG External Engagement Initiative Lead. Include a brief description of your challenge or interest, and we'll take it from there.
Q: What if my question isn't answered here?
Perfect. That's what conversations are for. Reach out with your question—we'd rather talk directly than leave you uncertain.
