Governance & Integration

This document describes how the Real-World Collaboration Procedure integrates into HAAG's existing governance structure and how it will be enforced and monitored over time. It's designed to support (not replace) existing HAAG processes.

Decision Authority & Approval Process

Partnership Approval Levels

The procedure incorporates several decision points where HAAG leadership must explicitly approve continuation:

Phase 1

Go/No-Go: Is this a partnership opportunity?

Authority: Faculty advisor + Initiative lead
Decision: Should we pursue scoping conversations?
Outcome: Approved or deferred (no shame in passing)

Phase 2

Go/No-Go: Is the scope approved?

Authority: Faculty advisor + Initiative lead + Department leadership
Decision: Does the partnership scope match HAAG capacity?
Outcome: Scope signed off or reshaped

Phase 3

Institutional Approval: Are agreements finalized?

Authority: Georgia Tech OTT + HAAG leadership + External partner
Decision: Are IP, data, budget, and confidentiality terms clear?
Outcome: Formal partnership agreement signed

Phase 4+

Ongoing Oversight: Is partnership on track?

Authority: Initiative lead (with faculty advisor)
Decision: Quarterly: Are we meeting goals? Need course correction?
Outcome: Continue, adjust scope, or wind down

Who Approves What?

Initiative Lead (Director of the Real-World Collaboration Initiative)

  • • Final authority on partnership fit and phase progression
  • • Manages quarterly reviews and course corrections
  • • Responsible for ensuring procedure is followed
  • • Escalates concerns to HAAG faculty leadership as needed

Faculty Advisor (Faculty overseeing research project)

  • • Recommends partnership fit for their research team
  • • Ensures academic standards are maintained
  • • Responsible for student mentorship and team management
  • • Provides input on scope feasibility

HAAG Leadership (Faculty leadership, department heads)

  • • Reviews major partnerships for strategic alignment
  • • Approves budget commitments and resource allocation
  • • Ensures compliance with Georgia Tech policies
  • • Provides guidance on high-risk or unusual partnerships

Georgia Tech Office of Technology Transfer

  • • Reviews IP ownership and licensing agreements
  • • Advises on patent protection and data confidentiality
  • • Drafts formal partnership and licensing agreements

Partnership Registry & Tracking

What Gets Tracked

All partnerships following this procedure should be registered in a HAAG partnership database. This serves multiple purposes:

  • Track which HAAG research projects are engaged in partnerships
  • Monitor outcomes and impact over time
  • Identify patterns and lessons learned
  • Ensure compliance with agreements and timelines
  • Share successes across the HAAG community

Partnership Registry Template

Each partnership record should include:

Basic Information

  • • Partnership ID (e.g., PART-2024-001)
  • • Partner organization name and contact
  • • HAAG faculty advisor
  • • Research team lead
  • • Partnership start date

Scope & Dates

  • • Research focus area (Urban Computing, Data Science, etc.)
  • • Expected duration and major milestones
  • • Scope approval date
  • • Partnership agreement signature date

Status & Oversight

  • • Current phase (Identification, Scoping, Building, Executing, Impact)
  • • Status (Active, On Track, At Risk, Paused, Completed)
  • • Last quarterly review date
  • • Next review scheduled

Outcomes (At Completion)

  • • Publications or presentations resulting from partnership
  • • Tools or artifacts delivered
  • • Impact on partner organization
  • • Capacity built (team, organization, field)
  • • Lessons learned and recommendations for future partnerships

Registry Maintenance

  • Updated quarterly: Initiative lead reviews active partnerships and updates status
  • Reviewed annually: HAAG leadership reviews all partnerships for alignment and impact
  • Shared semi-annually: Summary report provided to HAAG community (successes, lessons learned)
  • Used for planning: Registry informs decisions about resource allocation and new initiatives

Integration with Existing HAAG Structures

OMCS Program Alignment

The Real-World Collaboration Procedure supports the OMCS program's goals:

  • Student Learning: Partnerships provide real-world research experience for OMCS students
  • Research Rigor: Partnerships ground research in authentic problems while maintaining academic standards
  • Impact: Partnerships help OMCS demonstrate return on investment for students and funding bodies
  • Recruitment: Case studies and partnership opportunities attract high-quality students

Onboarding & Training

New HAAG faculty and researchers should understand this procedure as part of their onboarding:

For New Faculty

Briefing on real-world collaboration opportunities and the procedure for pursuing partnerships within HAAG

For New Researchers

Overview of current partnerships and how to engage with external collaborators if applicable to their projects

For Initiative Leaders

Comprehensive training on partnership approval, registry management, and quarterly oversight

Annual Review & Adaptation

The procedure itself should be reviewed and refined annually:

  1. Gather feedback from faculty, researchers, and external partners about what's working and what's not
  2. Review registry to identify patterns in successful partnerships and common challenges
  3. Assess alignment with HAAG and OMCS program goals and strategies
  4. Update procedure based on lessons learned and evolving needs
  5. Communicate changes clearly to the HAAG community

Risk Management & Enforcement

Common Risks & Mitigation

Risk: Partnership Scope Creep

Mitigation: Clear scope document, regular communication, and explicit "Phase 2" proposals for expanding scope. Initiative lead monitors during quarterly reviews.

Risk: Data Security or Confidentiality Breach

Mitigation: Georgia Tech OTT reviews all agreements upfront. Partnership agreement specifies data handling requirements. Quarterly reviews include data security check-in.

Risk: Partnership Fails to Deliver Value

Mitigation: Phase 2 scoping defines success metrics upfront. Phase 4 includes quarterly assessments with go/no-go decision points. Honest conversations about whether to continue or wind down.

Risk: Faculty/Researcher Overcommitment

Mitigation: Phase 1 explicitly assesses capacity. Scope is sized to available resources. If capacity issue emerges, adjust scope or bring in additional team members rather than pushing through.

Risk: Partner Drops Out Unexpectedly

Mitigation: Regular communication and quarterly reviews catch relationship issues early. Partnership agreement includes exit clauses and transition planning.

What Happens if the Procedure Isn't Followed?

The procedure is meant to support and strengthen partnerships, not to create bureaucracy. However, certain steps are non-negotiable:

  • Phase 1 Go/No-Go: Required before investing time in scoping
  • Phase 2 Scope Approval: Required before formal agreements drafted
  • Phase 3 Formal Agreement: Required before data access or budget commitment
  • Phase 4 Quarterly Reviews: Required for ongoing oversight

Projects that skip steps are at higher risk of misalignment, scope creep, or resource conflicts. The Initiative Lead should work with faculty to course-correct and ensure critical checkpoints are hit.

How We Know the Procedure is Working

Over time, we should see evidence that the procedure is supporting HAAG's mission and researcher success:

Partnership Health

Partnerships complete on schedule. Quarterly reviews show strong alignment and satisfaction from both sides

Research Quality

Publications in peer-reviewed venues. Research findings are rigorous and actionable

Real-World Impact

Partners implement recommendations. Tools are deployed and used. Problems are measurably improved

Student Learning

Students gain real-world research experience. Graduates point to partnerships as key to their education

Researcher Satisfaction

Faculty and researchers find partnerships rewarding. Repeat partnerships grow over time

Program Growth

More research groups engage in partnerships. Funding increases. HAAG's reputation as a partner grows
