What Is This Initiative?

HAAG projects are always academic, but sometimes have real world applications. Some of those projects can naturally evolve into external partnerships with cities, nonprofits, and organizations. This initiative documents and systematizes that process—creating a replicable framework for how academic research can collaborate with real-world partners to achieve impact.

We're developing this framework using the Charleston Trains case study as our proof of concept. The goal: help other HAAG projects identify collaboration opportunities and follow a documented process to build sustainable partnerships.

Why This Matters

Many academic research projects have real-world potential, and further, may be bolstered by being extended to real-world problems. But there's often no clear path from "good research idea" to "meaningful partnership." This initiative addresses that gap by providing:

  • A documented process for identifying collaboration opportunities
  • A replicable framework for scoping and launching partnerships
  • Real-world lessons from a successful case study
  • Guidance on funding external research collaborations

The Charleston Trains Project

The successful case study: Professor Mussmann and several HAAG researchers are working with the City of Charleston to predict train crossing blockages (a frequent and pressing issue in the city) in order to optimize traffic flows for residents, public transportation and emergency vehicles. This project demonstrates what's possible when academic research meets authentic civic challenges.
